2. Voice & Tone

Guidelines for how the brand speaks in text generation.

Primary Tone: Professional & Strategic. We sound like a trusted advisor—knowledgeable, calm, and forward-thinking. Avoid "hype" or overly aggressive marketing language.

Perspective: "We" (First person plural). We speak as a collective of experts partnering with our clients.

Complexity: Business Professional. Aim for a Grade 10–12 reading level. Use industry-standard terminology correctly, but avoid unnecessary jargon that obscures the core message.

Key Adjectives:

Authoritative: Confident in our data and insights.

Precise: Every word and data point must serve a clear, logical purpose.

Visionary: Focused on future growth and long-term stability.

3. Typography & Formatting

Guidelines for document structuring.

Primary Font: Inter (Bold/Semi-bold for headings).

Secondary/Pairing Font: Source Sans Pro (Regular for body text).

Formatting Rules:

Dates: DD Month YYYY (e.g., 06 April 2026) for international clarity.

Currency: Always include the currency code (e.g., USD $100 or EUR €100).

Oxford Comma: Yes. Use the Oxford comma in all lists to ensure professional precision.

Hierarchy: Use H2 for major sections and H3 for sub-sections. Never skip heading levels.

4. Visual Style & Imagery

Guidelines for image generation.

Image Style: Minimalist Photorealism. High-quality photography featuring clean lines, modern architecture, or professional environments. For illustrations, use Flat Vector styles with thin lines and no gradients.

Content Restrictions: No cartoons, no "stock photo" clichés (e.g., people shaking hands in front of a white wall), and no text inside images.

Mood: Sophisticated & Analytical. Lighting should be natural or cool (leaning into the blue/teal palette). Use a shallow depth of field to keep backgrounds uncluttered.

5. Negative Constraints (Do Not Use)

Explicit instructions on what the AI should avoid.

No Gradients: Do not use gradients in charts, icons, or UI elements; use flat hex colors only.

No Slang/Emojis: Strictly forbidden in all formal reports and client-facing copy.

No Clutter: Avoid "maximalist" designs. If a chart looks crowded, simplify the data density or split the visual.

No Decorative Borders: Visuals should be "borderless" and clean, relying on white space for separation.

No Warm Tones: Avoid heavy use of oranges, pinks, or purples unless they are required by the indicator color palette (Warning/Error).
